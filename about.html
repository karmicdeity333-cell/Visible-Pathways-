Why Visible Pathways Was Created

Visible Pathways was developed as part of a Phi Theta Kappa Honors in Action project at Pasco-Hernando State College.

Project Overview

Visible Pathways was created to help make educational opportunities and support systems more visible and accessible to individuals experiencing crisis or instability.

Throughout the research process, a recurring theme emerged: opportunities often exist, but many people never learn about them or are unsure how to access them.

Research Question

The research question that guided our project was "How can educational support systems be designed to make opportunities more visible and accessible to individuals experiencing crisis or instability?"

What We Learned

Research and community interviews suggested that opportunities are often difficult to find because information is scattered across multiple organizations, agencies, and systems.

Housing, transportation, food security, workforce development, financial aid, and educational opportunities are frequently presented separately, even though they are closely connected.

Fragmented Systems

One of the strongest findings of the project was that support systems are often fragmented.

Individuals seeking assistance may need to navigate multiple websites, organizations, and agencies simply to identify resources for which they may already qualify.

Visible Pathways was created to bring many of these resources together in one place.

Trusted Connections Matter

Community interviews suggested that people frequently learn about opportunities through trusted individuals and organizations rather than through independent searches.

Schools, community organizations, counselors, and support agencies often act as important bridges between individuals and available opportunities.

Why So Many Florida Resources?

Because this project was developed at Pasco-Hernando State College in Florida, many of the resources highlighted throughout the site focus on opportunities available within the state.

Whenever possible, national resources were also included so the information may remain valuable to visitors regardless of location.

Phi Theta Kappa Honors in Action

The Phi Theta Kappa Honors in Action (HIA) project is a team-based initiative where students combine academic research with community service to solve a real-world problem. Instead of just volunteering, students pick a research topic, study it using academic sources, and then design and execute a hands-on service project based directly on what they learned. It is designed to build leadership, project management, and critical thinking skills.

Resource Disclaimer

Visitors should consult the official websites of organizations and agencies for the most current information.
